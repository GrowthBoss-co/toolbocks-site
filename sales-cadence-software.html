Every step has an angle

Most cadence tools let you schedule "Email 3, Day 7" and leave the hard part, what that email should actually do, to whoever writes it. In ToolBocks, each step in a sequence carries a named psychological angle:

  • Pattern Interrupt to break through a cold inbox
  • Value Stack to make the offer concrete
  • Social Proof to borrow credibility from real results
  • Urgency to give a fence-sitter a reason to move
  • Breakup to close the loop and, surprisingly often, revive the deal

The result is a sequence a sales manager can read at a glance and know exactly what the prospect will experience over two weeks, instead of a stack of interchangeable "just following up" messages.

Email, SMS, and calls in one sequence

Cadences in ToolBocks are genuinely multi-channel. A single sequence can open with an email, follow with a text two days later, and drop the prospect into a dialer campaign for a live call at the right step. Because the dialer, blast tools, and cadence builder share one platform and one pipeline, a reply or a booked meeting stops the sequence automatically. No prospect gets a "checking in" email an hour after they booked.

AI drafts, humans approve

The platform's AI drafts every email and SMS from your business context, brand identity, and the step's angle. Nothing sends without a human approving it, and sends are paced so your domain and phone numbers behave like a person, not a cannon. It routes through your own GoHighLevel account, so deliverability reputation stays in your control.

Frequently asked questions

What channels can a cadence use?

Email, SMS, and call steps can be mixed in one multi-step sequence, with timing you control between steps.

Does AI write the cadence messages?

AI drafts every message using your business context, but a human approves each send — nothing goes out without sign-off.

What are the psychological angles?

Named frameworks each step can be built on — Pattern Interrupt, Value Stack, Social Proof, Urgency, and Breakup — so a cadence follows a deliberate persuasion arc instead of five generic follow-ups.
